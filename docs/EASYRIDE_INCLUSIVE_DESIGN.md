# PUI89 EasyRide AI Motorcycle — Inclusive Rider Design Brief

**Design direction:** a friendly, low-step-through electric motorcycle / premium urban scooter concept. The goal is to reduce physical effort and intimidation while retaining a robust, repairable, weather-conscious engineering approach.

**Status:** design proposal only. Dimensions, mass, handling, durability, safety functions and regulatory classification have not been validated.

## Design intent
Design for a broad range of adult riders, including women, older adults, shorter riders, new riders and people who prefer an easy-to-mount motorcycle. Do not assume all people have the same height, strength, balance, reach, vision or mobility.

## Proposed design features
- **Low step-through frame:** wide, unobstructed entry with no high leg swing; rounded edges and protected pinch points.
- **Skirt- and dress-conscious layout:** preserve a low, wide step-through opening so a rider can mount without a high leg swing; provide a broad, flat, non-slip footboard with clear foot placement; use smooth, snag-resistant side panels and a full chain/belt enclosure; shield hot or moving parts; avoid exposed hooks, sharp edges and protruding fasteners near clothing. Evaluate modesty, hem clearance and safe foot access while stationary and during mounting/dismounting. Never imply that every skirt length or garment is safe for riding.
- **Clothing compatibility is not a substitute for riding gear:** advise secure clothing and appropriate protective equipment; loose hems, scarves and long fabric can still catch in moving parts or wheels.
- **Upright neutral posture:** relaxed handlebar reach, natural wrist angle, low-effort steering geometry and a broad, supportive seat.
- **Adjustability:** evaluate more than one seat-height / reach configuration; controls should be reachable without stretching. Final ranges require ergonomic user trials.
- **Low centre of gravity:** package battery and heavy components low and centrally, while preserving ground clearance and service access.
- **Manageable size and mass:** prioritize compact dimensions and easy low-speed manoeuvring. Initial mass target for feasibility study: 95–110 kg excluding optional accessories; not a committed specification. A lighter vehicle must not compromise frame strength, braking, tyre capability or crash protection.
- **Confidence at low speed:** investigate hill-hold, walk-assist / reverse-assist and a stable stand. These are proposed features and require safety analysis; they are not implemented.
- **Visibility and usability:** large high-contrast display, plain-language warnings, tactile controls, clear indicators, bright lighting and mirrors with useful adjustment.
- **Weather and durability:** sealed connectors, drainage paths, corrosion-resistant fasteners, replaceable wear parts, impact-protected wiring and serviceable panels. No IP rating, waterproof or fire-resistance claim until tested.
- **Inclusive fit:** validate reach, mounting, foot placement, turning, parking and stopping for varied body sizes and abilities. Some riders may require adapted controls or a different vehicle class.

## AI assistance — rider remains in control
Potential software features:
1. Road-condition and hazard awareness with uncertainty labels.
2. Blind-zone / nearby-object advisory where sensor placement and performance are validated.
3. Vehicle-health monitoring for battery, electrical and thermal anomalies.
4. Simple visual and optional audio guidance with adjustable alert intensity.
5. Trip preparation: weather/context checklist, tyre/brake maintenance reminders and explainable status reports.
6. Data-integrity checks for missing, stale, noisy, conflicting or suspicious observations.

The AI must not independently steer, accelerate, brake, balance or override the rider. No autonomous riding or physical actuation is in scope.

## Safety and engineering requirements
- Research ABS and traction control as conventional vehicle safety systems; they require qualified vehicle engineering and homologation, not an unvalidated AI substitute.
- Define safe braking, tyre grip, centre-of-gravity, stability, steering effort, turning radius, stand strength, load rating and structural fatigue requirements.
- Test vibration, rain exposure, dust, corrosion, heat, cold, ingress, connector strain, drop/impact and serviceability under applicable standards.
- Analyse battery thermal runaway, electrical isolation, fuse protection, short circuit, charging faults and safe battery enclosure design.
- Conduct human-factors trials with diverse adult riders and qualified ergonomics / motorcycle engineering reviewers before making suitability claims.
- Keep all early work software-only and simulation-first. No connection to a real motorcycle, ECU, CAN bus, battery system or actuator.

## Proposed validation programme
1. Requirements and hazard analysis, including riders with varied height, strength, balance and experience.
2. Ergonomic mock-up evaluation: step-through clearance, seat reach, handlebar reach, foot placement and mounting effort.
3. Static structural and load analysis by qualified engineers.
4. Simulated low-speed manoeuvring, braking and traction scenarios.
5. Clothing-clearance and snag-risk review using varied skirt/dress lengths, fabrics and footwear; assess stationary mounting/dismounting and safe foot placement without fabric entering wheels or moving parts.
5. Controlled prototype testing only after design reviews, legal approval and qualified supervision.
6. Independent verification against applicable motorcycle regulations and standards in each target market.

## PUI89 zero-cost development gate
Use documentation, hand-authored SVGs, synthetic scenarios and existing free tools only. No paid APIs, subscriptions, cloud GPUs, paid datasets, new model downloads or new package installs without explicit approval. Check every tool and component licence, commercial-use terms and billing exposure before adoption.

## Claims policy
Use “concept”, “proposed”, “target”, “under study” and “not yet validated” where appropriate. Never claim universal suitability, guaranteed safety, crash prevention, waterproofing, fireproofing, certified performance or production readiness without independent evidence.
